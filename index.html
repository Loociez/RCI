🏁 Electric RC Basics

Electric RC cars are powered by brushless or brushed motors and rechargeable LiPo batteries. Lightweight, fast, and low maintenance, they are perfect for both beginners and experienced racers.

Motor Types

Brushed motors are simple and cheap. Brushless motors are more efficient and powerful. Sensored motors improve low-speed control.

ESC

Electronic speed controllers (ESCs) regulate motor speed and direction. Ensure your ESC matches your battery voltage and motor type.

Battery Tips

Select voltage and capacity suitable for your setup. Higher voltage = higher speed. Higher capacity = longer runtime.

🔧 Common RC Components

  • Chassis: The frame that holds everything together.
  • Motors: Brushless or brushed for propulsion.
  • ESC: Controls motor speed and braking.
  • Batteries: Usually LiPo packs, providing power to the system.
  • Servos: Control steering and other mechanical functions.
  • Radio Transmitter/Receiver: User control and telemetry.

💡 Beginner Tips

  • Start with brushed motors and basic ESCs to learn handling.
  • Always balance and safely charge LiPo batteries.
  • Check gearing ratios for your track type to avoid overheating.
  • Regularly inspect tires, suspension, and wires for wear.

🔋 LiPo Battery Info

Voltage per Cell

  • Full Charge: 4.2V
  • Nominal: 3.7V
  • Storage: 3.8V
  • Low Cutoff: 3.3V

C-Rating Formula

Capacity (Ah) × C-Rating = Max Safe Current (A)

Example: 5Ah × 30C = 150A max discharge. Higher C ratings allow stronger acceleration and less voltage sag.

Battery Capacity (mAh)

Capacity determines how long your RC vehicle can run.

  • 3000mAh – Short runtime
  • 5000mAh – Standard runtime
  • 7000mAh+ – Long runtime

Common LiPo Packs

  • 2S – 7.4V
  • 3S – 11.1V
  • 4S – 14.8V
  • 6S – 22.2V

Each LiPo cell provides 3.7V nominal voltage.

RC Battery Types

  • LiPo: Most powerful and common.
  • Li-Ion: Longer runtime but lower power.
  • NiMH: Older technology, very safe.

Charging Tips

  • Always use a balance charger.
  • Charge at 1C for best battery lifespan.
  • Allow batteries to cool before charging.
  • Charge in a LiPo safe bag when possible.

Storage Tips

  • Store at about 3.8V per cell.
  • Do not store fully charged batteries.
  • Keep batteries in a cool dry place.
  • Use your charger’s storage mode.

Safety

  • Stop using swollen batteries.
  • Never puncture or damage packs.
  • Avoid discharging below safe voltage.
  • Monitor temperature during use.

Performance Tips

  • Higher mAh = longer runtime.
  • Higher C rating = stronger acceleration.
  • Match battery voltage to ESC rating.
  • Avoid overheating batteries.

⚙️ KV & Voltage Calculator

What is Motor KV?

KV means RPM per Volt. It represents how many revolutions per minute a motor spins for each volt applied with no load.

Example: A 3500KV motor running on a 3S LiPo battery (11.1V) will spin roughly:

RPM = KV × Voltage
3500 × 11.1 = 38,850 RPM

This RPM is theoretical no-load speed. Real RPM under driving conditions is usually about 70–85% of this value due to drivetrain resistance and load.

What KV Affects

  • Top Speed: Higher KV motors spin faster and allow higher potential speed.
  • Torque: Lower KV motors produce more torque and are better for heavy vehicles.
  • Heat: High KV motors often draw more amps and generate more heat.
  • Battery Drain: Higher KV setups can drain batteries faster.

Setup Tips

  • 🔧 If you increase battery voltage, reduce pinion gear size.
  • 🔥 Keep motor temperatures below 80°C to prevent damage.
  • 🛞 Larger tires effectively increase gearing and may cause overheating.
  • ❄️ Use motor heatsinks or fans for high KV setups.
  • ⚙️ Rule of thumb: Large vehicle = lower KV

🌡️ ESC & Motor Guide

Temperature Guide

  • Safe ESC temperature: 40°C – 80°C
  • Safe motor temperature: 40°C – 90°C
  • Temperatures above this may damage electronics
  • If overheating occurs, stop driving and allow cooling

What an ESC Does

ESC stands for Electronic Speed Controller. It controls how much power from the battery is delivered to the motor.

  • Controls throttle and braking
  • Regulates motor speed
  • Protects electronics from damage
  • Supplies power to receiver via BEC

ESC Types

  • Brushed ESC – Used for brushed motors
  • Brushless ESC – Used for brushless motors
  • Sensored ESC – Smooth low speed control
  • Sensorless ESC – Simpler and more durable

Brushed vs Brushless

Brushed ESC

  • Cheaper
  • Simple design
  • Used in beginner vehicles

Brushless ESC

  • Much more powerful
  • Higher efficiency
  • Used in most modern RC vehicles

Sensored vs Sensorless

  • Sensored: smoother startup and precise throttle control
  • Sensorless: simpler, cheaper and more durable

Sensored setups are popular in racing and drifting. Sensorless setups are common in bashers and off-road vehicles.

ESC Amp Rating

ESCs are rated by the maximum current they can safely handle.

  • 1/10 vehicles: ~60A – 120A
  • 1/8 vehicles: ~120A – 200A
  • Large scale: 200A+

Always choose an ESC with higher amp capacity than your motor's expected draw.

BEC (Battery Eliminator Circuit)

The BEC powers your receiver and servo directly from the main battery.

  • Eliminates need for separate receiver battery
  • Typically provides 5V – 7.4V
  • Higher amp BECs are needed for powerful servos

Cooling Tips

  • Use ESC cooling fans
  • Ensure good airflow inside the chassis
  • Use heat sinks on motors
  • Avoid running in extreme heat conditions

Gearing & ESC Heat

  • Larger pinion gears increase motor load
  • High gearing can overheat ESCs
  • Reduce pinion size if temperatures rise
  • Larger tires also increase load

Warning Signs

  • ESC shutting down during driving
  • Thermal protection activating
  • Burning smell from electronics
  • Reduced performance after a few minutes

If these occur, stop driving and allow electronics to cool.

🛠️ RC Car Setup & Tips

Car Setup Tips

  • Adjust camber and toe angles for better cornering grip.
  • Tweak shock oil weight for smoothness on bumpy tracks.
  • Check wheel hexes and pinion/spur gear mesh for efficiency.
  • Fine-tune motor timing to balance speed vs. heat.

Maintenance Checklist

  • Clean chassis and suspension regularly.
  • Check screws, nuts, and body mounts.
  • Inspect and lubricate bearings and drivetrain.
  • Monitor ESC and motor temperature after runs.
  • Store LiPo batteries at proper voltage and temperature.

Track & Weather Guide

  • Use softer shocks on rough tracks, stiffer on smooth tracks.
  • High humidity can reduce traction; adjust tire inserts.
  • Wet tracks require waterproof electronics and careful throttle control.
  • Wind can affect lightweight cars; adjust gearing for stability.
